A True Retrospective Review of Game Of Thrones, Season 8


A lot of people tend to look back negatively on the final season of Game of Thrones, whether it's because of the choices that the showrunners made with deciding Daenerys' "Mad Queen" arch, or the way everything felt sped up, but I'd like to give a different take on this season.

Why does everyone "hate" this season so much?

Well, it seems as though a lot of people don't like this season because of a few top factors: the dialog between some of the best characters (Tyrion and Varys, most namely) really feels lack-luster and kinda like "locker room humor", when they were the most cleaverly written characters of the show. Factor 2: the timeline was extremely sped up. They would go from Dragonstone to Lannisport by ship (on the other side of the Westeros) within a matter of days, when it would take at least 3 months to get from Winterfell to Kings Landing.

But in this, I would like to give a different perspective on this season. Have we ever considered that what happened was bound to happen. That what Daenerys did wasn't abnormal for her, but the outcome of all of the other situations that led her to iron throne to begin with. That this was her, Bran's, and Jon Snow's destinies? If not then you should. Consider what happened in Essos- she was the queen of the Dothraki, the Khaleesi, and because King Robert was still trying to kill her- well after her father and brother were gone- her husband, Khal Drogo decided that he would do everything he could to get her back on the throne. If Robert had let it go, just as Ned Stark advised him to do, she would've been content with just being the Khaleesi to the Dothraki people. That was honestly the firdt domino to fall, in her short, heroic, and triumphant life.

Daenerys' next step, or well domino to fall was when she lost her husband, the Khal Drogo, and their unborn child. She lost her two most precious items and it was because of the "witch" that she believes that she saved. What she didn't know was that her Dothraki bloodriders as they're known had already abused her in multiple ways, along with the other women in that village after they captured it (robbed and pillaged might be te best term for the Dothraki's way of life). To Daenerys, she tried to "break that wheel", but unfortunately it costed her a lot. But it also gave her her dagons.

Time and again, we see her transform, and as she does she gains more braids showing her connection to her Dothraki people that she helped govern and whom she took with her on her journey- to signify the price she paid for all that she's getting now. It's also the thread throughout the seasons that shows the unspoken- she's a Conqueror now and she will break the wheel in Westeros.

Time and time again, she shows exactly what she means by Breaking the Wheel- she freed the slaves of Mareen, and they named her Mhysa (mother) for it. Mhysa, mother, something that the "witch" told her she would never be again, yet her she is proving her wrong. She was the mother of dragons and the mother of the freed people of Mareen. She crucified the masters of Mareen as they had some of their slaves. She fought injustice with justice- or at least what she had believed to be. By now, I believe she was still unknowingly showing signs of her Mad Queen capabilities, but it was always what seemed to be the right cause, until it felt like the right thing to do but without the forethought of consequences, from the people who are being taken out "justly" families and loved ones. People that she didn't know would be helping her. This caused the secret of Jon Snow's parentage being kept just that- a secret- would make her spiral so quickly. It's because she didn't have a perceived opponent that the people actually wanted.

By the time we are in season 8, she has already conquered multiple cities in Essos, and believed that she had the best judgement. When she comes to Westeros, it's not as she excepted. She was fed lies that the people of Westeros secretly made toasts to her rise and would accept her with open arms, but what she was met with was fear. Fear of a foreign queen coming with her Dothraki army, the Unsullied army, and 3 FULLY GROWN DRAGONS- they were rightfully terrified. Especially after the war of the Five Kings that just occurred, and the threat of the Long Night looming ahead. It was a powder keg ripe for explosion. And that's what ultimately happened. She lost her best friend and confidant and two of her dragons by the time that she decides to burn it all down. To her this was the only way to truly break the wheel, but through this final action, she didn't liberate the people of Kings Landing, or Westeros as a whole- she burned them all. For this, Jon Snow knew that there was only one solution for him, his family, and everyone in Westeros- he had to do it. And so he did.

It's extremely hard to take a look back at this with this new perspective, but ultimately this was going to happen. She had too much taken from her over her conquest and with each piece, it took a piece of her sanity with it, until she felt that she had nothing left.
